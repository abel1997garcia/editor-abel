The founder's story. Three. Why the founder started the brand is usually a powerful story that you can use in marketing to explain why someone should buy it from you. Brand consistency. Five. It's not that important unless you've been around for a while. You need to figure out what works first before you can decide what to become consistent on. Brand codes. Four. Don't spend six months designing brand code when no one knows who you are. But, if something does start to become recognizable you should lean into it and repeat it. Brand purpose. Nine. This is a huge mistake that a lot of brands make. Nobody cares about your brand's purpose, they only care about what your brand can do for them. Brand positioning. Two. You need to know what makes you different and why someone should choose you. Positioning is often connected to the founder's story. They work together. Your brand's name. Six. Not that important. There's so many successful brands. brands with stupid names. So as long as you're not called something really stupid or offensive, it doesn't really matter. Brand guidelines. Seven, not important unless you're a huge global brand. For small brands, you end up just spending six months agonizing and creating these things and it'll be ends up using them anyways. The product experience. The most important. In the early stages, your product is your brand. And if it sucks, then nothing else on this list is going to save you.
